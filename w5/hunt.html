The 93%

A textbook says:

“One study demonstrated that ML can be used not only to distinguish clinical populations from healthy controls but to differentiate between bipolar disorder and borderline personality disorder (Nazari et al., 2024): using EEG data and cognitive testing (Wisconsin Card Sorting Test and Integrated Cognitive Assessment), Nazari et al. attempted to discriminate between bipolar disorder type 2 (n=20) and borderline personality disorder (n=25) and the best model obtained an accuracy of 89%, a balanced accuracy of 93%, and an F1 score of 90 (Nazari et al., 2024).”

Jalali et al., Chapter 7, in Luxton (Ed.), Artificial Intelligence in Behavioral and Mental Health Care, 2nd edition, Elsevier, 2026, page 272.

Find the trick.

Facts from the paper and from the authors’ own computer code (the code and its printed results are posted online with the paper):

  1. The study took place at one clinic in Tehran, from June 2022 to March 2023.
  2. Code, step 2: split the 45 people at random. 36 people train the model. 9 people test it.
  3. 45 adults took part, aged 18 to 50: 25 with borderline personality disorder (BPD) and 20 with bipolar II disorder (BD II). A psychiatrist diagnosed each person with a structured interview based on DSM-5.
  4. The score in the abstract is balanced accuracy. The paper: “It is calculated as the average of sensitivity (true positive rate) and specificity (true negative rate)”. In plain words: the percentage correct in each group, then the average of the two.
  5. Each person had 10 minutes of resting EEG (21 electrodes) and two computer tests of thinking. The paper counts 318 measures per person.
  6. Code, step 3: try 29 different models (algorithms). Each one is trained on the 36 and tested on the 9.
  7. Table 1 of the paper. BPD: 21 women, 4 men. BD II: 7 women, 13 men. Mean age: BPD 30.6, BD II 31.9.
  8. The study was funded by Iran University of Medical Sciences.
  9. Code, step 1: choose the 11 measures that best separate the two groups (an ANOVA F-test on each measure). This step used all 45 people.
  10. The 9 test people: 7 with BPD, 2 with BD II.
  11. The abstract reports the top model: KNN (k-nearest neighbors).
  12. The 11 measures chosen: sex, and 10 EEG measures.

Paper: Nazari MJ, Shalbafan M, Eissazade N, et al. A machine learning approach for differentiating bipolar disorder type II and borderline personality disorder using electroencephalography and cognitive abnormalities. PLoS One. 2024;19(6):e0303699. Code: github.com/Mohammadjnazari/Bipolar-Borderline-Classification. PSY524, Week 5.
